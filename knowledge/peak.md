# Peak hours, load-shedding and demand response

Source notes written by Team JU_Segfault.

## What the peak is
The evening peak is the time of day when the grid carries the most load. In Bangladesh's summer it falls in the evening, around 7 to 11 PM, with the highest demand around 9 PM (IEEFA). When supply cannot meet the peak, distributors cut power to some areas, which is load-shedding.

## Why the peak is expensive
Meeting the last few hundred megawatts at the peak often needs the most expensive power plants. BPDB projected an average generation cost of about Tk 12.91 per unit for fiscal 2026-27 (EP-BD), above most residential tariff blocks, so every unit moved out of the peak helps.

## What demand response means
Demand response means asking users to reduce or shift electricity use for a short time when the grid is stressed, and rewarding them for it. Large programmes usually need smart meters. Bill Bachao does it with phone photos of ordinary meters: a reading before and after the challenge window shows what the home actually used.

## Shifting versus saving
Raising the AC set-point saves energy. Running the water pump, iron or washing machine after 11 PM does not save energy but moves it out of the peak. Bill Bachao counts these separately as kWh saved and kWh moved.

## Peak Hour Hero
On hot evenings Bill Bachao sends a voluntary challenge for the peak window, for example keeping the AC at 26 or 27 C and running heavy appliances after 11 PM. Users accept with one tap, photograph the meter at the start and end, and earn points. Area totals are shown to partners only as aggregates.

# Residential electricity tariff in Bangladesh (BERC, June 2026)

Source notes written by Team JU_Segfault from public reporting on the BERC order. Always check the rates printed on your own bill.

## How the residential slabs work
Residential (LT-A) electricity is billed in progressive blocks. Each block of units in a month is charged at its own rate, so only the units inside a higher block cost more, not the whole bill. From the June 2026 billing cycle the reported rates are: first 75 units Tk 5.26 per unit; units 76 to 200 Tk 8.50; units 201 to 300 Tk 9.10; units 301 to 400 Tk 9.62; units 401 to 600 Tk 15.01; above 600 units a higher top rate applies (Bill Bachao stores Tk 17.35 and asks users to verify it on their bill).
Sources: The Financial Express (power tariff adjustment, June 2026); Daily Observer (4 June 2026); Dhaka Tribune (units above the first 75 billed at Tk 8.50).

## The lifeline rate
Households whose total use in a month stays at or below 50 units are lifeline consumers. All of their units are billed at the lifeline rate of Tk 4.63 per unit. Going above 50 units moves the household onto the normal blocks.

## The 400-unit jump
The biggest price step is at 400 units. A unit inside the 301-400 block costs Tk 9.62, but every unit above 400 costs Tk 15.01, about 56 percent more. Reporting in August 2026 said many households saw higher bills because rising use pushed them into higher slabs, especially above 600 units. Staying just under 400 units can therefore save a noticeable amount.

## VAT and the demand charge
A 5 percent VAT is added on top of the energy charge. Bills also carry a demand charge based on the sanctioned load in kilowatts; the exact amount is printed on the bill and Bill Bachao lets the user enter it.

## How Bill Bachao uses the tariff
Bill Bachao never asks an AI model to calculate a bill. A tested tariff function applies the blocks, the lifeline rule, the demand charge and VAT, and the coach only explains the result.
